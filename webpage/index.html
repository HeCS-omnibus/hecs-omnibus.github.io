HeCS-omnibus

A spectroscopic compilation of 227 galaxy clusters in the local universe.

Two decades of MMT/Hectospec and SDSS spectroscopy, reprocessed into one catalog: caustic membership, dynamical masses, and galaxy properties for clusters at 0.02 < z < 0.30.

What it is

One catalog, many surveys

HeCS-omnibus gathers two decades of cluster spectroscopy taken with the 300-fiber Hectospec on the 6.5 m MMT — the Hectospec Cluster Survey and its X-ray, Sunyaev–Zeldovich and red-sequence selected extensions — together with SDSS redshifts and the CIRS infall-region sample, then reprocesses everything homogeneously.

Each cluster typically has about 180 spectroscopic members identified with the caustic technique, ranging from 16 to 1,209. From those members we derive velocity dispersions, R200 and M200, and identify the brightest cluster galaxy.

The compilation was assembled by Sohn, Geller, Diaferio & Rines (2020) to study the connection between brightest cluster galaxies and their host halos, and it now serves as the parent sample for deeper follow-up.

How the catalog is built

Spectroscopy
Hectospec: 300 fibers over a one-degree field, 3700–9100 Å, R ~ 1500–1700, reduced with HSRed and cross-correlated with RVSAO (RXC > 3). SDSS spectra for r < 17.77, supplemented by ACReS, OmegaWINGS and NED where available.
Membership
The caustic technique (Diaferio & Geller 1997; Diaferio 1999) traces the escape-velocity envelope in redshift space. In simulations it recovers about 90% of true members within 3 R200 with fewer than 8% interlopers.
Cluster properties
M200 and R200 from the caustic mass profile; velocity dispersion of members within R200 using the bi-weight estimator, with uncertainties from 10,000 bootstrap resamplings.
Galaxy properties
Stellar masses from Le Phare SED fits to SDSS ugriz (BC03 models, Chabrier IMF); Dn4000 from the spectra; central stellar velocity dispersions from SDSS/Portsmouth and Hectospec/ULySS, aperture-corrected to 3 kpc.
BCG identification
The brightest r-band member within 0.5 R200, then checked against SDSS images — about 25 clusters needed correction for crowded-field photometry.
Cosmology
H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7.

Catalog

What is in it

227 clusters

Identifier, position, redshift, number of caustic members overall and within R200, velocity dispersion, R200 and M200.

BCG catalog

SDSS object ID, position, redshift, r-band magnitude, stellar mass, Dn4000 and central stellar velocity dispersion.

52,325 members

Redshifts from Hectospec and SDSS with derived absolute magnitude, Dn4000, stellar mass and central stellar velocity dispersion.

Most clusters have velocity dispersions above 400 km s−1 and dynamical masses above 1014 M☉; less massive systems appear only at z ≲ 0.1 because of Malmquist bias.

Going deeper

Curious how far the spectroscopy can go?

MACH takes the nine most massive, best-sampled clusters in HeCS-omnibus and observes them to completion — more than 4,500 spectra each, no color selection, stellar mass functions two dex deeper than SDSS.
