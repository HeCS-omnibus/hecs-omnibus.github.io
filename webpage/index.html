HeCS-omnibus

A spectroscopic compilation of 227 galaxy clusters in the local universe.

Built from MMT/Hectospec and SDSS spectroscopy, HeCS-omnibus provides caustic-based membership, dynamical masses, and galaxy properties for clusters at 0.02 < z < 0.30. Its densest subset, MACH, targets the nine most massive clusters nearby with complete spectroscopy.

What it is

One catalog, many surveys

HeCS-omnibus gathers two decades of cluster spectroscopy with the 300-fiber Hectospec on the 6.5 m MMT — the Hectospec Cluster Survey and its X-ray, Sunyaev–Zeldovich and red-sequence selected extensions — together with SDSS redshifts and the CIRS infall-region sample, then reprocesses everything homogeneously.

Each cluster typically has ~180 spectroscopic members identified with the caustic technique, from which we derive velocity dispersions, R200 and M200, and identify the brightest cluster galaxy. For member galaxies we provide redshifts, Dn4000, stellar masses and central stellar velocity dispersions.

The compilation was assembled by Sohn, Geller, Diaferio & Rines (2020) to study the connection between BCGs and their host clusters, and now serves as the parent sample for deeper follow-up.

How the catalog is built

Spectroscopy
Hectospec: 300 fibers over a 1° field, 3700–9100 Å, R ~ 1500–1700, reduced with HSRed and cross-correlated with RVSAO (RXC > 3). SDSS spectra for r < 17.77, supplemented by ACReS, OmegaWINGS and NED where available.
Membership
The caustic technique (Diaferio & Geller 1997; Diaferio 1999) traces the escape-velocity envelope in redshift space. In simulations it recovers ~90% of true members within 3 R200 with < 8% interlopers.
Cluster properties
M200 and R200 from the caustic mass profile; velocity dispersion of members within R200 with the bi-weight estimator and 10,000 bootstrap resamplings.
Galaxy properties
Stellar masses from Le Phare SED fits to SDSS ugriz (BC03, Chabrier IMF); Dn4000 from the spectra; central stellar velocity dispersions from SDSS/Portsmouth and Hectospec/ULySS, aperture-corrected to a 3 kpc aperture.
BCG identification
Brightest r-band member within 0.5 R200, then visually checked against SDSS images — about 25 clusters needed correction for crowded-field photometry.
Cosmology
H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3, ΩΛ = 0.7.

MACH

The Massive Cluster Survey with Hectospec

Deep, dense and complete spectroscopy of the nine most massive galaxy clusters in the local universe.

MACH selects the most massive, best-sampled clusters in HeCS-omnibus at 0.07 < z < 0.11 — a redshift window chosen so that R200 fits inside the one-degree Hectospec field. Between 2017 and 2023 we obtained 41,897 unique Hectospec spectra, targeting every galaxy brighter than r = 21.3 with no color selection.

The result is a sample that is spectroscopically complete to r ≈ 20.1–21.1 within each cluster, with 400–1,400 members per system and stellar mass functions that reach log(M*/M☉) ≈ 8.5, roughly two dex deeper than SDSS in the same volume.

  1. Complete. More than 4,500 spectra per cluster and a 90% completeness limit of r ≈ 20.1–21.1. Because there is no color selection, quiescent and star-forming galaxies are sampled to the same depth.
  2. Massive. M200 from 5.5 × 1014 to 1.0 × 1015 M☉, with a typical mass of 7.4 × 1014 M☉.
  3. Deep. Stellar mass functions complete to log M* > 9 without any correction, and extended to log M* ≈ 8.5 with an empirical completeness correction.

What we have learned so far

Cluster vs. field

Between log M* = 10.5 and 11.4 the cluster SMF has the same shape as the field but twice the amplitude. Above 11.4 the clusters show a clear excess of massive galaxies, including the BCGs.

Quiescent vs. star-forming

Quiescent galaxies follow a curved SMF peaking near log M* ≈ 10.5; star-forming galaxies rise monotonically toward low mass. In the cluster core the quiescent peak shifts to lower mass.

Quenching toward the center

The quiescent fraction rises toward the cluster center at every stellar mass, and most steeply for low-mass galaxies.

Against IllustrisTNG-300

Simulated clusters of the same mass match the observed SMF at 10.5–11.4, but host too-massive BCGs and about half as many galaxies at log M* = 9–10.5.
